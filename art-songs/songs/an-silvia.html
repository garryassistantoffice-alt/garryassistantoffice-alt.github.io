An Silvia

To Silvia · Franz Schubert, D. 891, Op. 106 No. 4 (1826)
Poem: Eduard von Bauernfeld (1802–1890), German translation of Shakespeare, The Two Gentlemen of Verona (Act IV, scene 2)

Was ist Silvia, saget an,
Daß sie die weite Flur preist?
Schön und zart seh' ich sie nahn,
Auf Himmels Gunst und Spur weist,
Daß ihr alles untertan.
Who is Silvia, tell me,
that the wide countryside praises her?
Lovely and delicate I see her approach;
she bears the sign of heaven's favour,
so that all things are subject to her.
Ist sie schön und gut dazu?
Reiz labt wie milde Kindheit;
Ihrem Aug' eilt Amor zu,
Dort heilt er seine Blindheit
Und verweilt in süßer Ruh'.
Is she lovely and good as well?
Charm refreshes like gentle childhood;
Cupid hurries to her eyes,
there he cures his blindness
and lingers in sweet rest.
Darum Silvia, tön', o Sang,
Der holden Silvia Ehren;
Jeden Reiz besiegt sie lang,
Den Erde kann gewähren:
Kränze ihr und Saitenklang!
So, O song, ring out for Silvia,
for gracious Silvia's honour;
she has long surpassed every charm
that earth can give:
garlands for her, and the sound of strings!
About the song. The serenade 'Who is Silvia?' from The Two Gentlemen of Verona, set by Schubert in 1826 in a German translation by his friend Eduard von Bauernfeld. Some lines of the translation are hard to construe: 'Reiz labt wie milde Kindheit' (literally 'charm refreshes like gentle childhood') stands where Shakespeare has 'For beauty lives with kindness'.
