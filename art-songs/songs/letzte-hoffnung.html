Letzte Hoffnung

Last Hope · Franz Schubert, Winterreise, D. 911, No. 16 (1827)
Poem: Wilhelm Müller (1794–1827)

Hie und da ist an den Bäumen
Manches bunte Blatt zu sehn,
Und ich bleibe vor den Bäumen
Oftmals in Gedanken stehn.
Here and there on the trees
a few coloured leaves can be seen,
and I often stop in front of the trees
and stand lost in thought.
Schaue nach dem einen Blatte,
Hänge meine Hoffnung dran;
Spielt der Wind mit meinem Blatte,
Zittr' ich, was ich zittern kann.
I look at one particular leaf
and hang my hope on it;
if the wind plays with my leaf,
I tremble with all my might.
Ach, und fällt das Blatt zu Boden,
Fällt mit ihm die Hoffnung ab;
Fall' ich selber mit zu Boden,
Wein' auf meiner Hoffnung Grab.
Ah, and if the leaf falls to the ground,
my hope falls away with it;
I fall to the ground with it myself
and weep on the grave of my hope.
About the song. The traveller fixes his hope on one of the last coloured leaves left on the trees; if it falls, his hope falls with it. Schubert set 'manches bunte Blatt' (many a coloured leaf) where Müller has 'noch ein buntes Blatt' (still a coloured leaf), and he repeats 'wein'' in the last line. 'Zittr' ich, was ich zittern kann' means 'I tremble as hard as I can'.
