Cäcilie

Cecily · Richard Strauss, Op. 27, No. 2 (1894)
Poem: Heinrich Hart (1855–1906)

Wenn du es wüßtest,
was träumen heißt von brennenden Küssen,
von Wandern und Ruhen mit der Geliebten,
Aug' in Auge,
und kosend und plaudernd,
wenn du es wüßtest,
du neigtest dein Herz!
If you knew
what it is to dream of burning kisses,
of wandering and resting with the beloved,
eye to eye,
caressing and talking,
if you knew,
you would incline your heart!
Wenn du es wüßtest,
was bangen heißt in einsamen Nächten,
umschauert vom Sturm, da niemand tröstet
milden Mundes die kampfmüde Seele,
wenn du es wüßtest,
du kämest zu mir.
If you knew
what it is to be afraid in lonely nights,
with the storm shuddering around, when no one comforts
with gentle mouth the battle-weary soul,
if you knew,
you would come to me.
Wenn du es wüßtest,
was leben heißt, umhaucht von der Gottheit
weltschaffendem Atem,
zu schweben empor, lichtgetragen,
zu seligen Höhn,
wenn du es wüßtest,
du lebtest mit mir!
If you knew
what it is to live, breathed upon by the Godhead's
world-creating breath,
to soar upward, borne by light,
to blissful heights,
if you knew,
you would live with me!
About the song. Hart wrote the poem for his wife Cäcilie, whose name appears only in the title; each stanza says what love could be 'if you knew'. 'Umschauert vom Sturm' means with the storm shuddering all around; 'lichtgetragen' means borne up by light. The modern OpenScore transcription reads 'und schauert vom Sturm'; Aibl's print has 'umschauert'.
