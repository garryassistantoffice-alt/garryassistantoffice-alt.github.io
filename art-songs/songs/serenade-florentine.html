Sérénade florentine

Florentine Serenade · Henri Duparc, Mélodie
Poem: Jean Lahor (Henri Cazalis, 1840–1909), from L’illusion

Étoile, dont la beauté luit
Comme un diamant dans la nuit,
Regarde vers ma bien-aimée
Dont la paupière s'est fermée,
Et fais descendre sur ses yeux
La bénédiction des cieux.
Star, whose beauty shines
like a diamond in the night,
look toward my beloved,
whose eyelids have closed,
and bring down upon her eyes
the blessing of the heavens.
Elle s'endort... Par la fenêtre
En sa chambre heureuse pénètre;
Sur sa blancheur, comme un baiser,
Viens jusqu'à l'aube te poser,
Et que sa pensée, alors, rêve
D'un astre d'amour qui se lève!
She is falling asleep... Through the window
make your way into her happy room;
on her whiteness, like a kiss,
come and rest until dawn,
and then may her thoughts dream
of a star of love that is rising!
About the song. A lover asks the evening star to watch over his sleeping beloved and to send her a dream of love. Lahor's poem, printed with the same title in L'illusion (1893), begins 'Étoile, dont la clarté luit' (whose brightness shines); Duparc sings 'dont la beauté luit' (whose beauty shines). The 'astre d'amour' of the last line is the star of love, Venus as evening star.
