Danksagung an den Bach

Thanks to the Brook · Franz Schubert, Die schöne Müllerin, D. 795, No. 4 (1823)
Poem: Wilhelm Müller (1794–1827)

War es also gemeint,
Mein rauschender Freund,
Dein Singen, dein Klingen,
War es also gemeint?
So is this what you meant,
my rushing friend,
your singing, your ringing,
is this what you meant?
Zur Müllerin hin!
So lautet der Sinn.
Gelt, hab' ich's verstanden?
Zur Müllerin hin!
To the miller's daughter!
That is what it means.
I have understood it, haven't I?
To the miller's daughter!
Hat sie dich geschickt?
Oder hast mich berückt?
Das möcht' ich noch wissen,
Ob sie dich geschickt.
Did she send you?
Or have you bewitched me?
That is what I would still like to know:
whether she sent you.
Nun wie's auch mag sein,
Ich gebe mich drein:
Was ich such', hab' ich funden,
Wie's immer mag sein.
Well, however it may be,
I give myself up to it:
what I was looking for, I have found,
however it may be.
Nach Arbeit ich frug,
Nun hab' ich genug,
Für die Hände, für's Herze
Vollauf genug!
I asked for work,
now I have enough,
for my hands, for my heart,
enough and to spare!
About the song. He thanks the brook for leading him to the miller's daughter, 'die Müllerin', and wonders whether she sent it or whether it simply charmed him. 'Gelt' is a southern German tag meaning 'isn't that so?'; 'berückt' means bewitched or beguiled; 'funden' is an old form of 'gefunden' (found) and 'frug' of 'fragte' (asked).
