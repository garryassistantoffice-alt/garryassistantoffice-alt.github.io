À Chloris

To Chloris · Reynaldo Hahn, Mélodie
Poem: Théophile de Viau (1590–1626)

S'il est vrai, Chloris, que tu m'aimes,
(Mais j'entends, que tu m'aimes bien,)
Je ne crois pas que les rois mêmes
Aient un bonheur pareil au mien.
Que la mort serait importune
À venir changer ma fortune
Pour la félicité des cieux!
Tout ce qu'on dit de l'ambroisie
Ne touche point ma fantaisie
Au prix des grâces de tes yeux.
If it is true, Chloris, that you love me
(and by that I mean, that you love me dearly),
I do not believe that even kings
have a happiness equal to mine.
How unwelcome death would be
if it came to exchange my lot
for the bliss of heaven!
All that is said of ambrosia
does not stir my fancy
compared with the graces of your eyes.
About the song. Hahn sets the first stanza of a love poem by the 17th-century poet Théophile de Viau, with a piano part in the style of a Baroque aria; the last three lines are sung twice, printed once. Viau's 1856 edition reads 'Ayent un heur comme le mien', 'De venir' and 'A la félicité des dieux'; the score has the modernised words printed here. 'J'entends' means 'I mean' (not 'I hear'), 'fantaisie' is fancy or desire, and 'au prix de' means 'compared with'.
