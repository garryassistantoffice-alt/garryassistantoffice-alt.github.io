Le colibri

The Hummingbird · Ernest Chausson, Op. 2 No. 7
Poem: Charles-Marie Leconte de Lisle (1818–1894), from Poèmes barbares

Le vert colibri, le roi des collines,
Voyant la rosée et le soleil clair
Luire dans son nid tissé d'herbes fines,
Comme un frais rayon s'échappe dans l'air.
The green hummingbird, the king of the hills,
seeing the dew and the bright sun
shining in his nest woven of fine grasses,
darts into the air like a fresh ray of light.
Il se hâte et vole aux sources voisines
Où les bambous font le bruit de la mer,
Où l'açoka rouge, aux odeurs divines,
S'ouvre et porte au cœur un humide éclair.
He hurries and flies to the nearby springs
where the bamboos make the sound of the sea,
where the red ashoka, with its heavenly scents,
opens and carries a moist gleam to the heart.
Vers la fleur dorée il descend, se pose,
Et boit tant d'amour dans la coupe rose,
Qu'il meurt, ne sachant s'il l'a pu tarir.
To the golden flower he descends, alights,
and drinks so much love from the rosy cup
that he dies, not knowing whether he could drain it.
Sur ta lèvre pure, ô ma bien-aimée,
Telle aussi mon âme eût voulu mourir
Du premier baiser qui l'a parfumée!
On your pure lip, O my beloved,
so too my soul would have wished to die
of the first kiss that perfumed it!
About the song. A sonnet in which a hummingbird dies of drinking too much love from a flower, and the poet wishes he had died the same way at his beloved's first kiss. Chausson sets it in a swaying 5/4 metre and keeps the poem unchanged. The 'açoka' is the ashoka, a flowering tree of India; 'eût voulu mourir' means 'would have wished to die'.
