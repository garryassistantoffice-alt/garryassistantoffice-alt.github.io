Amarilli, mia bella

Amarilli, My Lovely One · Giulio Caccini, Le nuove musiche (Florence, 1602)
Poem: Anonymous

Amarilli, mia bella,
non credi, o del mio cor dolce desio,
d'esser tu l'amor mio?
Credilo pur: e se timor t'assale,
dubitar non ti vale.
Aprimi il petto e vedrai scritto in core:
Amarilli è il mio amore.
Amarilli, my lovely one,
do you not believe, O sweet desire of my heart,
that you are my love?
Do believe it: and if fear assails you,
doubting will not help you.
Open my breast and you will see written on my heart:
Amarilli is my love.

Caccini's original text (1602)

As printed in Le nuove musiche (Florence, 1602). The two lines that differ from the anthology text are lines 5 and 6.

Amarilli, mia bella,
non credi, o del mio cor dolce desio,
d'esser tu l'amor mio?
Credilo pur: e se timor t'assale,
prendi questo mio strale,
aprimi il petto e vedrai scritto il core:
Amarilli è il mio amore.
Amarilli, my lovely one,
do you not believe, O sweet desire of my heart,
that you are my love?
Do believe it: and if fear assails you,
take this arrow of mine,
open my breast and you will see my heart inscribed:
Amarilli is my love.
About the song. A solo madrigal from Caccini's Le nuove musiche (Florence, 1602), the collection that launched the new expressive solo song; Amarilli is a conventional shepherdess name of pastoral poetry. We print both versions: first the text of the Parisotti/Schirmer anthology that most singers use, then Caccini's own 1602 text, which differs in two lines: 'prendi questo mio strale' (take this arrow of mine) where the anthology has 'dubitar non ti vale' (literally 'to doubt does not avail you'), and 'scritto il core' (my heart written) for 'scritto in core'.
