Recueillement

Meditation · Claude Debussy, Cinq poèmes de Baudelaire, No. 4 (1887–89)
Poem: Charles Baudelaire (1821–1867), from Les fleurs du mal

Sois sage, ô ma Douleur, et tiens-toi plus tranquille.
Tu réclamais le Soir; il descend; le voici:
Une atmosphère obscure enveloppe la ville,
Aux uns portant la paix, aux autres le souci.
Be good, O my Sorrow, and keep quieter.
You were asking for the Evening; it is falling; here it is:
a dim atmosphere wraps the city,
bringing peace to some, to others care.
Pendant que des mortels la multitude vile,
Sous le fouet du Plaisir, ce bourreau sans merci,
Va cueillir des remords dans la fête servile,
Ma Douleur, donne-moi la main; viens par ici,
While the vile multitude of mortals,
under the whip of Pleasure, that merciless executioner,
goes to gather remorse at the servile feast,
my Sorrow, give me your hand; come this way,
Loin d'eux. Vois se pencher les défuntes Années,
Sur les balcons du ciel, en robes surannées;
Surgir du fond des eaux le Regret souriant;
away from them. See the dead Years lean out
from the balconies of the sky, in old-fashioned gowns;
see smiling Regret rise from the depths of the waters;
Le Soleil moribond s'endormir sous une arche,
Et, comme un long linceul traînant à l'Orient,
Entends, ma chère, entends la douce Nuit qui marche.
the dying Sun fall asleep beneath an arch,
and, like a long shroud trailing in the East,
hear, my dear, hear the gentle Night walking.
About the song. A sonnet in which the poet speaks to his Sorrow as to a restless child and leads it away from the crowd's pleasures into the evening. 'Recueillement' is the gathering-in of the mind, contemplation. The 1868 edition prints the capitals kept here (Douleur, Soir, Plaisir, Années, Regret, Soleil, Nuit), which make these into persons.
