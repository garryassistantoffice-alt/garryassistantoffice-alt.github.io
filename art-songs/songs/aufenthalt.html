Aufenthalt

Dwelling Place · Franz Schubert, Schwanengesang, D. 957, No. 5 (1828)
Poem: Ludwig Rellstab (1799–1860)

Rauschender Strom,
Brausender Wald,
Starrender Fels
Mein Aufenthalt.
Rushing river,
roaring forest,
rigid rock:
my dwelling place.
Wie sich die Welle
An Welle reiht,
Fließen die Tränen
Mir ewig erneut.
As wave
follows on wave,
so my tears flow,
forever renewed.
Hoch in den Kronen
Wogend sich's regt,
So unaufhörlich
Mein Herze schlägt.
As high in the treetops
there is a surging stir,
so ceaselessly
my heart beats.
Und wie des Felsen
Uraltes Erz,
Ewig derselbe
Bleibet mein Schmerz.
And like the rock's
age-old ore,
for ever the same
my pain remains.
About the song. The poet names the wild landscape as his home and finds his own state in each part of it: tears as endless as the waves, a heart as restless as the treetops, a pain as unchanging as the rock. The song returns to the first stanza at the end. 'Aufenthalt' means a place where one stays, an abode; 'Erz' is ore, the metal in the rock.
