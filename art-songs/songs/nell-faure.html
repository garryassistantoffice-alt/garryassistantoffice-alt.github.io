Nell

Nell · Gabriel Fauré, Op. 18, No. 1
Poem: Charles-Marie Leconte de Lisle (1818–1894), from Poèmes antiques

Ta rose de pourpre, à ton clair soleil,
Ô Juin, étincelle enivrée;
Penche aussi vers moi ta coupe dorée:
Mon cœur à ta rose est pareil.
Your crimson rose, in your bright sun,
O June, sparkles as if drunk;
tilt your golden cup toward me too:
my heart is like your rose.
Sous le mol abri de la feuille ombreuse
Monte un soupir de volupté;
Plus d'un ramier chante au bois écarté,
Ô mon cœur, sa plainte amoureuse.
Under the soft shelter of the shady leaves
a sigh of delight rises;
more than one wood pigeon in the far-off wood sings,
O my heart, its lament of love.
Que ta perle est douce au ciel enflammé,
Étoile de la nuit pensive!
Mais combien plus douce est la clarté vive
Qui rayonne en mon cœur charmé!
How sweet your pearl is in the blazing sky,
star of the pensive night!
But how much sweeter is the living light
that shines in my enchanted heart!
La chantante mer, le long du rivage,
Taira son murmure éternel,
Avant qu'en mon cœur, chère amour, ô Nell,
Ne fleurisse plus ton image!
The singing sea, along the shore,
will silence its eternal murmur
before, in my heart, dear love, O Nell,
your image ceases to bloom!
About the song. The June rose, the wood pigeon's song, the evening star and the sea are all outdone by the lover's heart, where Nell's image will bloom until the sea falls silent. The poem is the third of Leconte de Lisle's 'Chansons écossaises' (Scottish songs) in Poèmes antiques; Fauré sings 'au ciel enflammé' (in the blazing sky) for the poet's 'au ciel parfumé' (in the fragrant sky). 'Ramier': a wood pigeon.
