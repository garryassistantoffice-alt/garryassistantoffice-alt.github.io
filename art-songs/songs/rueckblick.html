Rückblick

Looking Back · Franz Schubert, Winterreise, D. 911, No. 8 (1827)
Poem: Wilhelm Müller (1794–1827)

Es brennt mir unter beiden Sohlen,
Tret' ich auch schon auf Eis und Schnee,
Ich möcht' nicht wieder Atem holen,
Bis ich nicht mehr die Türme seh'.
The ground burns under both my soles,
though I am already treading on ice and snow;
I would not draw breath again
until I can no longer see the towers.
Hab' mich an jeden Stein gestoßen,
So eilt' ich zu der Stadt hinaus;
Die Krähen warfen Bäll' und Schloßen
Auf meinen Hut von jedem Haus.
I have stumbled on every stone,
so fast did I hurry out of the town;
the crows threw snowballs and hailstones
onto my hat from every house.
Wie anders hast du mich empfangen,
Du Stadt der Unbeständigkeit!
An deinen blanken Fenstern sangen
Die Lerch' und Nachtigall im Streit.
How differently you received me,
you town of inconstancy!
At your gleaming windows
lark and nightingale sang in contest.
Die runden Lindenbäume blühten,
Die klaren Rinnen rauschten hell,
Und ach, zwei Mädchenaugen glühten! –
Da war's gescheh'n um dich, Gesell!
The round linden trees were in bloom,
the clear runnels babbled brightly,
and ah, a girl's two eyes were glowing!
Then it was all over with you, my fellow!
Kömmt mir der Tag in die Gedanken,
Möcht' ich noch einmal rückwärts seh'n,
Möcht' ich zurücke wieder wanken,
Vor ihrem Hause stille steh'n.
When that day comes into my thoughts,
I would like to look back once more,
I would like to stagger back again
and stand still before her house.
About the song. The traveller has fled the town so fast that he stumbled on every stone, pelted by the crows; then he remembers how different his arrival was, in spring, and longs to turn back and stand once more before her house. 'Schloßen' are hailstones; 'Rinnen' are the gutters or runnels of the streets; 'Gesell' is a journeyman, here simply 'fellow'. 'Kömmt' is an older form of 'kommt'.
