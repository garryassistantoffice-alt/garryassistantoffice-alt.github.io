Danza, danza, fanciulla gentile

Dance, Dance, Gentle Girl · Attributed to Francesco Durante, Arietta (melody from a Durante solfeggio; words added later)
Poem: Anonymous (19th century?)

Danza, danza, fanciulla gentile,
al mio cantar;
gira leggera, sottile al suono,
al suono dell'onde del mar.
Dance, dance, gentle girl,
to my singing;
turn lightly, nimbly, to the sound,
to the sound of the waves of the sea.
Senti il vago rumore
dell'aura scherzosa
che parla al core
con languido suon,
e che invita a danzar senza posa,
che invita a danzar.
Hear the lovely murmur
of the playful breeze
that speaks to the heart
with a languid sound,
and invites you to dance without rest,
invites you to dance.
About the song. The melody appears to come from one of Durante's wordless singing exercises (solfeggi); the Italian words and the piano part were added later, probably in the 19th century (the tenor Lorenzo Pagans has been suggested as the author of the words), so the song as sung is not an 18th-century piece. 'Vago' means lovely, not vague; 'sottile' is literally 'thin, slender', here 'nimbly'.
