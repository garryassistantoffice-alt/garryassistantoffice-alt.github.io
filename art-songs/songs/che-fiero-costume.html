Che fiero costume

What a Cruel Habit · Giovanni Legrenzi, Arietta (publ. 1678 in Echi di riverenza, Op. 14)
Poem: Anonymous

Che fiero costume
d'aligero nume,
che a forza di pene
si faccia adorar!
What a cruel habit
of the winged god,
that by force of pain
he makes himself adored!
E pur nell'ardore
il dio traditore
un vago sembiante
mi fe' idolatrar.
And yet in my passion
the treacherous god
made me worship
a lovely face.
Che crudo destino
che un cieco bambino
con bocca di latte
si faccia stimar!
What a harsh fate,
that a blind child
with a mouth still full of milk
should make himself respected!
Ma questo tiranno
con barbaro inganno
entrando per gli occhi
mi fe' sospirar.
But this tyrant,
with barbarous deceit,
entering through my eyes,
made me sigh.
About the song. The singer complains of the habit of the winged god, Cupid, who makes himself adored by inflicting pain, and of the 'blind child' with a 'mouth of milk' (a baby still at the breast) who must be taken seriously. 'Aligero nume' is a poetic phrase for 'winged god'. Parisotti's anthology (1894) and Floridia's Early Italian Songs (1923) both name Legrenzi's opera Eteocle (Eteocle e Polinice) as the source; the piece was first printed in his collection Echi di riverenza, Op. 14 (1678), and was later used in a revival of the opera.
