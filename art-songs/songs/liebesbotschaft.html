Liebesbotschaft

Love's Message · Franz Schubert, Schwanengesang, D. 957, No. 1 (1828)
Poem: Ludwig Rellstab (1799–1860)

Rauschendes Bächlein,
So silbern und hell,
Eilst zur Geliebten
So munter und schnell?
Ach, trautes Bächlein,
Mein Bote sei du;
Bringe die Grüße
Des Fernen ihr zu.
Rushing little brook,
so silvery and bright,
are you hurrying to my beloved
so merrily and fast?
Ah, dear little brook,
be my messenger;
bring her the greetings
of the one far away.
All' ihre Blumen
Im Garten gepflegt,
Die sie so lieblich
Am Busen trägt,
Und ihre Rosen
In purpurner Glut,
Bächlein, erquicke
Mit kühlender Flut.
All her flowers,
tended in the garden,
which she wears so charmingly
at her breast,
and her roses
in their crimson glow:
little brook, refresh them
with your cooling water.
Wenn sie am Ufer,
In Träume versenkt,
Meiner gedenkend
Das Köpfchen hängt,
Tröste die Süße
Mit freundlichem Blick,
Denn der Geliebte
Kehrt bald zurück.
When on the bank,
sunk in dreams,
thinking of me,
she lets her head droop,
comfort my sweet girl
with a friendly glance,
for her beloved
will soon return.
Neigt sich die Sonne
Mit rötlichem Schein,
Wiege das Liebchen
In Schlummer ein.
Rausche sie murmelnd
In süße Ruh,
Flüstre ihr Träume
Der Liebe zu.
When the sun goes down
with a reddish glow,
rock my darling
to sleep.
Murmuring, lull her
into sweet rest,
whisper to her
dreams of love.
About the song. The first song of Schwanengesang: a lover far from home asks the brook that runs past his sweetheart's garden to carry his greetings, refresh her roses, comfort her and murmur her to sleep. 'Traut' means dear, intimate; 'des Fernen' is 'of the one far away'. In Rellstab's collected poems (1844) the second line ends with an exclamation mark; the Schubert prints read it as a question.
