La pastorella dell'Alpi

The Shepherd Girl of the Alps · Gioachino Rossini, Soirées musicales, No. 6 (publ. 1835)
Poem: Carlo Pepoli (1796–1881)

Son bella pastorella
che scende ogni mattino,
ed offre un cestellino
di fresche frutta e fior.
I am a pretty shepherd girl
who comes down each morning
and offers a little basket
of fresh fruit and flowers.
Chi viene al primo albore
avrà vezzose rose
e poma rugiadose:
venite al mio giardin!
Ahu! ahu!
Whoever comes at first dawn
will have charming roses
and apples wet with dew:
come to my garden!
Ahu! ahu!
Chi nel notturno orrore
smarrì la buona via,
alla capanna mia
ritroverà il cammin.
Whoever, in the dread of night,
has lost the right road
will find the way again
at my hut.
Venite, o passaggiero,
la pastorella è qua;
ma il fior del suo pensiero
ad uno sol darà!
Ahu! ahu!
Come, O traveller,
the shepherd girl is here;
but the flower of her thoughts
she will give to one man only!
Ahu! ahu!
About the song. Headed 'Tirolese' in the Troupenas edition: an Alpine song, with the yodel-like cry 'ahu!' after each verse. The girl sells fruit and flowers and offers the lost traveller her hut, but the 'flower of her thoughts' is kept for one man only. 'Poma' is an old plural of 'pomo', apple or fruit. Rossini repeats the first verse after the second; we print it once.
