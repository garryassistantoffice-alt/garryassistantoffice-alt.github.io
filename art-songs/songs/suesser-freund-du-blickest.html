Süßer Freund, du blickest

Sweet Friend, You Look at Me · Robert Schumann, Frauenliebe und -leben, Op. 42, No. 6 (1840)
Poem: Adelbert von Chamisso (1781–1838)

Süßer Freund, du blickest
mich verwundert an,
kannst es nicht begreifen,
wie ich weinen kann;
laß der feuchten Perlen
ungewohnte Zier
freudig hell erzittern
in dem Auge mir.
Sweet friend, you look
at me in wonder;
you cannot understand
how I can weep;
let the unaccustomed adornment
of these moist pearls
tremble, joyful and bright,
in my eye.
Wie so bang mein Busen,
wie so wonnevoll!
Wüßt' ich nur mit Worten,
wie ich's sagen soll;
komm und birg dein Antlitz
hier an meiner Brust,
will ins Ohr dir flüstern
alle meine Lust.
How anxious my heart is,
how full of bliss!
If only I knew
how to put it into words;
come and hide your face
here on my breast;
I want to whisper into your ear
all my joy.
Weißt du nun die Tränen,
die ich weinen kann,
sollst du nicht sie sehen,
du geliebter Mann?
Bleib' an meinem Herzen,
fühle dessen Schlag,
daß ich fest und fester
nur dich drücken mag.
Now that you know the tears
that I can weep,
should you not see them,
my beloved husband?
Stay close to my heart,
feel how it beats,
so that I may hold you
closer and closer, only you.
Hier an meinem Bette
hat die Wiege Raum,
wo sie still verberge
meinen holden Traum;
kommen wird der Morgen,
wo der Traum erwacht,
und daraus dein Bildnis
mir entgegenlacht.
Here beside my bed
there is room for the cradle,
where it may quietly hide
my sweet dream;
the morning will come
when the dream awakes
and your likeness
smiles up at me from it.
About the song. The young wife weeps for joy and tells her husband, whispering on his breast, that she is expecting a child: there is room for a cradle by her bed. Schumann leaves out Chamisso's third stanza, in which she says her mother has explained the signs to her. Chamisso wrote 'Freudenhell erzittern / In den Wimpern mir' (tremble, bright with joy, on my lashes), and ends the line 'Du geliebter Mann' with a semicolon where Schumann's print has a question mark. 'Birg' is the imperative of 'bergen', to hide.
