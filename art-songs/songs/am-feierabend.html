Am Feierabend

After Work · Franz Schubert, Die schöne Müllerin, D. 795, No. 5 (1823)
Poem: Wilhelm Müller (1794–1827)

Hätt' ich tausend
Arme zu rühren!
Könnt' ich brausend
Die Räder führen!
Könnt' ich wehen
Durch alle Haine!
Könnt' ich drehen
Alle Steine!
Daß die schöne Müllerin
Merkte meinen treuen Sinn!
If only I had a thousand
arms to work!
If only I could drive
the wheels with a roar!
If only I could blow
through all the groves!
If only I could turn
all the millstones!
So that the lovely miller's daughter
would notice my faithful heart!
Ach, wie ist mein Arm so schwach!
Was ich hebe, was ich trage,
Was ich schneide, was ich schlage,
Jeder Knappe tut mir's nach.
Und da sitz' ich in der großen Runde,
In der stillen, kühlen Feierstunde,
Und der Meister spricht zu Allen:
Euer Werk hat mir gefallen;
Und das liebe Mädchen sagt
Allen eine gute Nacht.
Ah, how weak my arm is!
What I lift, what I carry,
what I cut, what I hammer,
any mill-hand can do as well as I.
And there I sit in the big circle
in the quiet, cool hour of rest,
and the master says to us all:
'Your work has pleased me';
and the dear girl says
good night to everyone.
About the song. 'Feierabend' is the end of the working day. The young man wishes for a thousand arms so that the miller's daughter would notice him, but in the evening the master praises everyone alike and she says good night to them all. A 'Knappe' is a mill-hand; Müller wrote 'Jeder Knappe tut es nach' (every lad does the same), Schubert 'tut mir's nach' (does as well as I). Schubert repeats the first stanza at the end.
