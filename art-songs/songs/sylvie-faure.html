Sylvie

Sylvie · Gabriel Fauré, Op. 6, No. 3
Poem: Paul de Choudens (1850–1925)

Si tu veux savoir, ma belle,
Où s'envole à tire-d'aile
L'oiseau qui chantait sur l'ormeau?
Je te le dirai, ma belle:
Il vole vers qui l'appelle,
Vers celui-là
Qui l'aimera!
If you want to know, my lovely,
where it flies off on swift wings,
the bird that was singing on the young elm,
I shall tell you, my lovely:
it flies toward whoever calls it,
toward the one
who will love it!
Si tu veux savoir, ma blonde,
Pourquoi sur terre et sur l'onde
La nuit tout s'anime et s'unit?
Je te le dirai, ma blonde:
C'est qu'il est une heure au monde
Où, loin du jour,
Veille l'amour!
If you want to know, my fair-haired one,
why on land and on the waves
everything at night comes alive and unites,
I shall tell you, my fair-haired one:
it is because there is an hour in the world
when, far from the day,
love keeps watch!
Si tu veux savoir, Sylvie,
Pourquoi j'aime à la folie
Tes yeux brillants et langoureux?
Je te le dirai, Sylvie:
C'est que sans toi dans la vie
Tout pour mon cœur
N'est que douleur!
If you want to know, Sylvie,
why I love to distraction
your bright and languorous eyes,
I shall tell you, Sylvie:
it is because without you in life
everything, for my heart,
is nothing but sorrow!
About the song. Three questions and three answers, each addressed to the girl by a different name (my lovely, my fair one, Sylvie), lead from a bird's flight to the singer's own love. The poet, Paul de Choudens, was also a music publisher and opera librettist. 'Ormeau': a young elm; 'à tire-d'aile': with rapid wingbeats, as fast as wings can go.
