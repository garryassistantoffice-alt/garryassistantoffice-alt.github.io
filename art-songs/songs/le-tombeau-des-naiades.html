Le tombeau des Naïades

The Tomb of the Naiads · Claude Debussy, Trois chansons de Bilitis, No. 3 (1897–98)
Poem: Pierre Louÿs (1870–1925), from Les chansons de Bilitis

Le long du bois couvert de givre, je marchais;
mes cheveux devant ma bouche
se fleurissaient de petits glaçons,
et mes sandales étaient lourdes
de neige fangeuse et tassée.
Along the frost-covered wood I was walking;
my hair in front of my mouth
was blossoming with little icicles,
and my sandals were heavy
with muddy, trampled snow.
Il me dit: « Que cherches-tu?
— Je suis la trace du satyre.
Ses petits pas fourchus alternent
comme des trous dans un manteau blanc. »
He said to me: 'What are you looking for?'
'I am following the satyr's track.
His little cloven footprints alternate
like holes in a white cloak.'
Il me dit: « Les satyres sont morts.
Les satyres et les nymphes aussi.
Depuis trente ans il n'a pas fait un hiver aussi terrible.
La trace que tu vois est celle d'un bouc.
Mais restons ici, où est leur tombeau. »
He said to me: 'The satyrs are dead.
The satyrs and the nymphs too.
In thirty years there has not been so terrible a winter.
The track you see is a he-goat's.
But let us stay here, where their tomb is.'
Et avec le fer de sa houe
il cassa la glace de la source
où jadis riaient les naïades.
Il prenait de grands morceaux froids,
et, les soulevant vers le ciel pâle,
il regardait au travers.
And with the iron of his hoe
he broke the ice of the spring
where the naiads once laughed.
He took up great cold pieces,
and, lifting them toward the pale sky,
he looked through them.
About the song. A winter walk in which Bilitis looks for the satyr's track and her companion tells her that satyrs and nymphs are dead. 'Je suis' here is from 'suivre' (I follow), not 'être'. Naiads are the nymphs of springs and streams; a 'houe' is a hoe, and the 'fer' its iron blade.
