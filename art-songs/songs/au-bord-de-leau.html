Au bord de l'eau

By the Water · Gabriel Fauré, Mélodie
Poem: Sully Prudhomme (1839–1907), from Les vaines tendresses

S'asseoir tous deux au bord du flot qui passe,
Le voir passer;
Tous deux, s'il glisse un nuage en l'espace,
Le voir glisser;
To sit together by the stream that flows past,
to watch it pass;
together, if a cloud glides through space,
to watch it glide;
A l'horizon, s'il fume un toit de chaume,
Le voir fumer;
Aux alentours, si quelque fleur embaume,
S'en embaumer;
if on the horizon a thatched roof smokes,
to watch it smoke;
if some flower nearby gives off its scent,
to be filled with its scent;
Entendre au pied du saule où l'eau murmure
L'eau murmurer;
Ne pas sentir, tant que ce rêve dure,
Le temps durer;
to hear, at the foot of the willow where the water murmurs,
the water murmur;
not to feel, while this dream lasts,
time going on;
Mais n'apportant de passion profonde
Qu'à s'adorer,
Sans nul souci des querelles du monde,
Les ignorer;
but, bringing deep passion
only to adoring each other,
with no care for the quarrels of the world,
to know nothing of them;
Et seuls, tous deux devant tout ce qui lasse,
Sans se lasser,
Sentir l'amour, devant tout ce qui passe,
Ne point passer!
and alone together, in the face of all that wearies,
never to grow weary;
to feel love, in the face of all that passes,
never pass away!
About the song. A long sentence of infinitives ('to sit', 'to watch', 'to feel') that pictures two lovers at rest beside a stream while everything else passes. Fauré leaves out the poet's fifth stanza (fruit and birdsong), sings 'au bord du flot' for 'au bord d'un flot', and 'seuls, tous deux' for 'seuls, heureux'. 'Flot' is the flowing water of the stream, not a flood.
