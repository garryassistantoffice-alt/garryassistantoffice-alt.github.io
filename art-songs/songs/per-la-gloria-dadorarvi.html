Per la gloria d'adorarvi

For the Glory of Adoring You · Giovanni Battista Bononcini, Griselda (London, 1722)
Poem: Paolo Rolli (1687–1765), after Apostolo Zeno

Per la gloria d'adorarvi
voglio amarvi, o luci care.
Amando penerò,
ma sempre v'amerò,
sì, nel mio penare.
For the glory of adoring you
I want to love you, O dear eyes.
Loving, I shall suffer,
but I shall always love you,
yes, in my suffering.
Senza speme di diletto
vano affetto è sospirare,
ma i vostri dolci rai
chi vagheggiar può mai
e non v'amare?
Without hope of delight
sighing is a vain affection,
but your sweet eyes,
who could ever gaze on them
and not love you?
About the song. An aria from Bononcini's London opera Griselda, written for the Royal Academy of Music, where he was Handel's rival. The singer accepts that loving without hope means suffering, and loves anyway. 'Luci' (literally 'lights') and 'rai' (rays) are both poetic words for the beloved's eyes; 'vagheggiar' means to gaze at fondly.
