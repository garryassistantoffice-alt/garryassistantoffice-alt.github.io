Prison

Prison · Gabriel Fauré, Op. 83, No. 1 (1894)
Poem: Paul Verlaine (1844–1896), from Sagesse

Le ciel est, par-dessus le toit,
Si bleu, si calme!
Un arbre, par-dessus le toit,
Berce sa palme.
The sky, above the roof,
is so blue, so calm!
A tree, above the roof,
rocks its leafy crown.
La cloche, dans le ciel qu'on voit,
Doucement tinte.
Un oiseau sur l'arbre qu'on voit
Chante sa plainte.
The bell, in the sky that one sees,
gently rings.
A bird on the tree that one sees
sings its lament.
Mon Dieu, mon Dieu, la vie est là,
Simple et tranquille.
Cette paisible rumeur-là
Vient de la ville.
My God, my God, life is there,
simple and quiet.
That peaceful murmur there
comes from the town.
Qu'as-tu fait, ô toi que voilà
Pleurant sans cesse,
Dis, qu'as-tu fait, toi que voilà,
De ta jeunesse?
What have you done, O you there,
weeping without end?
Say, what have you done, you there,
with your youth?
About the song. From Verlaine's Sagesse (Book III, No. VI); Fauré gave it the title 'Prison'. Through a window the speaker sees sky, tree and bell tower, hears the town, and turns on himself with the last question. 'Palme' is a palm-like spray of leaves; 'rumeur' is a distant murmur of sound, not a rumour. Fauré sets the poem as printed.
