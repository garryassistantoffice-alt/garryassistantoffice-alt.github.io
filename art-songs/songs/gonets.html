Гонец

The Messenger · Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov, Four Romances, Op. 4 No. 2 (1866)
Poem: Mikhail Mikhailov (1829–1865), after Heinrich Heine (1797–1856)

Вставай, слуга! Коня седлай!
Чрез рощи и поля
Скачи скорее ко дворцу
Дункана-короля!
Get up, servant! Saddle the horse!
Through groves and fields
gallop quickly to the palace
of King Duncan!
Зайди в конюшню там и жди!
И если кто войдёт,
Спроси: которую Дункан
Дочь замуж отдаёт?
Go into the stable there and wait!
And if anyone comes in,
ask: which of his daughters
is Duncan giving in marriage?
Коль чернобровую — лети
Во весь опор назад!
Коль ту, что с русою косой, —
Спешить не надо, брат!
If it is the dark-browed one, fly
back at full gallop!
If it is the one with the fair braid,
there is no need to hurry, brother!
Тогда ступай на рынок ты,
Купи верёвку там;
Вернися шагом и молчи:
Я угадаю сам.
Then go to the market,
buy a rope there;
come back at a walk and say nothing:
I will guess for myself.
About the song. Mikhailov's Russian version of a Heine poem. A lover sends his servant to King Duncan's court to learn which daughter is to be married: if it is the dark-browed one, the servant must gallop back; if it is the fair-haired one, there is no hurry, and he should buy a rope on the way home. The rope is for the lover to hang himself. 'Русая коса' is a light-brown or fair braid. The song is dedicated to Prince Alexander Dmitrievich Myshetsky.
