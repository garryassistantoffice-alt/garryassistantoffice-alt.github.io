Il mio ben quando verrà

When Will My Beloved Come · Giovanni Paisiello, Aria, from the opera Nina, o sia La pazza per amore (1789)
Poem: Giovanni Battista Lorenzi, after Giuseppe Carpani's translation of Marsollier

Il mio ben quando verrà
a veder la mesta amica?
Di bei fior s'ammanterà
la spiaggia aprica.
Ma nol vedo,
e il mio ben, ahimè! non vien?
When will my beloved come
to see his sorrowful friend?
With lovely flowers will be clothed
the sunny shore.
But I do not see him,
and my beloved, alas, does he not come?
Mentre all'aure spiegherà
la sua fiamma, i suoi lamenti,
miti augei, v'insegnerà
più dolci accenti.
Ma non l'odo.
E chi l'udì?
Ah! il mio bene ammutolì.
While he pours out to the breezes
his passion, his laments,
gentle birds, he will teach you
sweeter songs.
But I do not hear him.
And who has heard him?
Ah, my beloved has fallen silent.
Tu cui stanca omai già fe'
il mio pianto, eco pietosa,
ei ritorna e dolce a te
chiede la sposa.
Pian, mi chiama; piano, ahimè!
No, non mi chiama, oh Dio, non c'è.
You, whom by now my weeping
has already wearied, kind echo,
he is coming back, and sweetly of you
he asks for his bride.
Softly, he is calling me; softly, alas!
No, he is not calling me, oh God, he is not there.
About the song. Nina, driven mad by grief when her lover is thought killed in a duel, waits every day for him to return. She hears nothing, asks the echo to call him, and imagines he calls her. A Milan libretto of 1804 reads 'Ma nol vedo... ma sospiro', 'Mille, o augei' and 'e forse a te'; the text here is as set in Parisotti's anthology. 'Augei' is poetic for birds; 'aprica' means sunny.
