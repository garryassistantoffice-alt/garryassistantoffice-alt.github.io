Le voyageur

The Traveller · Gabriel Fauré, Op. 18, No. 2
Poem: Armand Silvestre (1837–1901)

Voyageur, où vas-tu, marchant
Dans l'or vibrant de la poussière?
— Je m'en vais au soleil couchant,
Pour m'endormir dans la lumière.
Traveller, where are you going, walking
in the quivering gold of the dust?
'I am going toward the setting sun,
to fall asleep in the light.
Car j'ai vécu n'ayant qu'un Dieu,
L'astre qui luit et qui féconde,
Et c'est dans son linceul de feu
Que je veux m'en aller du monde!
For I have lived with only one God,
the star that shines and makes the earth fruitful,
and it is in its shroud of fire
that I want to leave the world!'
— Voyageur, presse donc le pas:
L'astre vers l'horizon décline...
— Que m'importe, j'irai plus bas
L'attendre au pied de la colline.
'Traveller, then quicken your step:
the star is sinking toward the horizon...'
'What does it matter to me? I shall go further down
to wait for it at the foot of the hill.
Et lui montrant mon cœur ouvert,
Saignant de son amour fidèle,
Je lui dirai: j'ai trop souffert,
Soleil! emporte-moi loin d'elle!
And, showing it my open heart,
bleeding from its faithful love,
I shall say to it: I have suffered too much;
Sun, carry me far away from her!'
About the song. A dialogue on the road: a traveller walking into the sunset explains that he worships only the sun and wants to die in its light, far from the woman who made him suffer. The text is read from an early Hamelle print of the song and agrees word for word with the poem in Silvestre's collection Les ailes d'or (poems of 1878–1880). 'L'astre' (the star) is the sun throughout; 'féconder': to make fertile.
