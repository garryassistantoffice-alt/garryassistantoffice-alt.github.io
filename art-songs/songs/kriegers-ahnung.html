Kriegers Ahnung

Warrior's Foreboding · Franz Schubert, Schwanengesang, D. 957, No. 2 (1828)
Poem: Ludwig Rellstab (1799–1860)

In tiefer Ruh' liegt um mich her
Der Waffenbrüder Kreis;
Mir ist das Herz so bang und schwer,
Von Sehnsucht mir so heiß.
In deep repose, all around me,
lies the circle of my comrades in arms;
my heart is so anxious and heavy,
so burning with longing.
Wie hab' ich oft so süß geträumt
An ihrem Busen warm!
Wie freundlich schien des Herdes Glut,
Lag sie in meinem Arm!
How often I have dreamt so sweetly
on her warm breast!
How friendly the fire on the hearth seemed
when she lay in my arms!
Hier, wo der Flammen düstrer Schein
Ach! nur auf Waffen spielt,
Hier fühlt die Brust sich ganz allein,
Der Wehmut Träne quillt.
Here, where the flames' gloomy glow
plays, alas, only on weapons,
here my heart feels utterly alone,
a tear of sadness wells up.
Herz! daß der Trost dich nicht verläßt!
Es ruft noch manche Schlacht.
Bald ruh' ich wohl und schlafe fest,
Herzliebste – gute Nacht!
Heart, may comfort not desert you!
Many a battle is still calling.
Soon I shall rest, I think, and sleep soundly:
dearest love, good night!
About the song. A soldier lies awake among his sleeping comrades by the camp fire, remembering the hearth at home and his sweetheart, and senses that he will soon sleep for good. 'Ahnung' is a foreboding, a presentiment; 'Waffenbrüder' are brothers in arms. Rellstab wrote 'Wie hab' ich oft so süß geruht' (rested); both the Haslinger and the Peters prints have Schubert's 'geträumt' (dreamt), and 'der Flammen' for Rellstab's 'der Flamme'. Schubert repeats the last stanza.
