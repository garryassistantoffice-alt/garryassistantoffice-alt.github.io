Spesso vibra per suo gioco

Often, for His Sport · Alessandro Scarlatti, La caduta de' Decemviri (Naples, 1697)
Poem: Silvio Stampiglia (1664–1725)

Spesso vibra per suo gioco
il bendato pargoletto
strali d'oro in umil petto,
stral di ferro in nobil core.
Often, for his sport,
the blindfolded little boy hurls
golden arrows into a humble breast,
an iron arrow into a noble heart.
Poi languendo in mezzo al foco
del diverso acceso strale,
per oggetto non eguale
questo manca e quel vien meno.
Then, languishing in the midst of the fire
lit by the different arrows,
for a beloved who is no match for them,
this one swoons and that one wastes away.
About the song. The 'blindfolded little boy' is Cupid, who shoots golden arrows into humble hearts and iron ones into noble hearts, so that lovers of unequal rank pine for each other. The words are an aria text in Stampiglia's libretto La caduta de' Decemviri, which Scarlatti set in Naples in 1697. The libretto reads 'in nobil seno' (in a noble breast), which the score uses when the opening returns; the first time the anthology has 'in nobil core'.
