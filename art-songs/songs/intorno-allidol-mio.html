Intorno all'idol mio

Around My Idol · Antonio Cesti, Orontea (Innsbruck, 1656)
Poem: Giacinto Andrea Cicognini (1606–c. 1651)

Intorno all'idol mio
spirate pur, spirate,
aure soavi e grate,
e nelle guance elette
baciatelo per me,
cortesi aurette!
Around my idol
blow, yes, blow,
gentle and pleasing breezes,
and on his exquisite cheeks
kiss him for me,
kind little breezes!
Al mio ben, che riposa
su l'ali della quiete,
grati sogni assistete,
e il mio racchiuso ardore
svelategli per me,
o larve d'amore!
My beloved, who rests
on the wings of quiet,
attend, pleasant dreams,
and my hidden passion
reveal to him for me,
O phantoms of love!
About the song. Queen Orontea sings this lullaby-like aria over Alidoro, the young painter she loves, as he rests, asking the breezes to kiss him and the dreams to reveal her love. 'Larve' means phantoms or apparitions (here dream figures), not larvae; 'guance elette' is literally 'chosen cheeks', i.e. exquisite, matchless cheeks.
