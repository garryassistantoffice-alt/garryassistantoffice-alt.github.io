Frühlingsglaube

Faith in Spring · Franz Schubert, D. 686, Op. 20 No. 2 (1820, revised 1822)
Poem: Ludwig Uhland (1787–1862)

Die linden Lüfte sind erwacht,
Sie säuseln und weben Tag und Nacht,
Sie schaffen an allen Enden.
O frischer Duft, o neuer Klang!
Nun, armes Herze, sei nicht bang!
Nun muß sich alles, alles wenden.
The gentle breezes have awoken,
they whisper and weave day and night,
they are at work everywhere.
O fresh scent, O new sound!
Now, poor heart, do not be afraid!
Now everything, everything must change.
Die Welt wird schöner mit jedem Tag,
Man weiß nicht, was noch werden mag,
Das Blühen will nicht enden.
Es blüht das fernste, tiefste Tal:
Nun, armes Herz, vergiß der Qual!
Nun muß sich alles, alles wenden.
The world grows lovelier with every day,
no one knows what may yet come,
the blossoming will not end.
The farthest, deepest valley is in bloom:
now, poor heart, forget your torment!
Now everything, everything must change.
About the song. Spring is at work everywhere, and the poet tells his poor heart that now everything must change for the better. 'Linden' here is the adjective 'lind' (gentle, mild), not the linden tree. We print 'säuseln und weben' (whisper and weave), Uhland's word, which the old Breitkopf & Härtel complete Schubert edition also prints, as does Gustav Engel's 1884 quotation of the melody. Many performing editions (Peters, Schirmer, Ditson) print 'wehen' (blow) instead; Max Friedlaender's own note in the Peters edition says that reading is found neither in Schubert nor in Uhland.
