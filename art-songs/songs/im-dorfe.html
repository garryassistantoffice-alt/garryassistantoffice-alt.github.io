Im Dorfe

In the Village · Franz Schubert, Winterreise, D. 911, No. 17 (1827)
Poem: Wilhelm Müller (1794–1827)

Es bellen die Hunde, es rasseln die Ketten;
Es schlafen die Menschen in ihren Betten,
Träumen sich manches, was sie nicht haben,
Tun sich im Guten und Argen erlaben:
The dogs are barking, the chains are rattling;
people are asleep in their beds,
dreaming of many things they do not have,
taking their pleasure in good and bad:
Und morgen früh ist alles zerflossen.
Je nun, sie haben ihr Teil genossen
Und hoffen, was sie noch übrig ließen,
Doch wieder zu finden auf ihren Kissen.
and early tomorrow it has all melted away.
Well, well, they have had their share,
and they hope to find again on their pillows
whatever they still left over.
Bellt mich nur fort, ihr wachen Hunde,
Laßt mich nicht ruh'n in der Schlummerstunde!
Ich bin zu Ende mit allen Träumen.
Was will ich unter den Schläfern säumen?
Just bark me away, you wakeful dogs,
do not let me rest in the hour of slumber!
I am finished with all dreams.
Why should I linger among the sleepers?
About the song. At night the village dogs bark and rattle their chains while the villagers sleep and dream of what they do not have; the traveller, done with dreaming, asks the dogs to drive him on. 'Sich erlaben' is to refresh oneself, to take delight; 'je nun' is a shrug, 'well, well'; 'säumen' is to linger. Müller's villagers snore ('Die Menschen schnarchen'); Schubert's sleep.
