La rose

The Rose · Gabriel Fauré, Op. 51, No. 4
Poem: Charles-Marie Leconte de Lisle (1818–1894), from Poèmes antiques

Je dirai la rose aux plis gracieux.
La rose est le souffle embaumé des Dieux,
Le plus cher souci des Muses divines.
Je dirai ta gloire, ô charme des yeux,
Ô fleur de Kypris, reine des collines!
I shall sing of the rose with its graceful folds.
The rose is the fragrant breath of the gods,
the dearest care of the divine Muses.
I shall sing your glory, O delight of the eyes,
O flower of Kypris, queen of the hills!
Tu t'épanouis entre les beaux doigts
De l'Aube écartant les ombres moroses;
L'air bleu devient rose et roses les bois;
La bouche et le sein des vierges sont roses!
You open out between the lovely fingers
of Dawn as she parts the gloomy shadows;
the blue air turns rose, and rose the woods;
the mouths and breasts of maidens are rose!
Heureuse la vierge aux bras arrondis
Qui dans les halliers humides te cueille!
Heureux le front jeune où tu resplendis!
Heureuse la coupe où nage ta feuille!
Happy the maiden with rounded arms
who gathers you in the damp thickets!
Happy the young brow where you shine!
Happy the cup in which your petal floats!
Ruisselante encor du flot paternel,
Quand de la mer bleue Aphrodite éclose
Étincela nue aux clartés du ciel,
La Terre jalouse enfanta la rose;
Et l'Olympe entier, d'amour transporté,
Salua la fleur avec la Beauté!
When, still streaming from the paternal wave,
Aphrodite, just born from the blue sea,
gleamed naked in the light of the sky,
jealous Earth gave birth to the rose;
and all Olympus, carried away by love,
hailed the flower together with Beauty!
About the song. A hymn in the Greek manner to the rose, flower of Aphrodite, born on earth when the goddess rose from the sea. Fauré sings 'La bouche et le sein des vierges' (of maidens) where Leconte de Lisle wrote 'des Nymphes'. Kypris (of Cyprus) is Aphrodite; the 'flot paternel' (paternal wave) is the sea from which she was born.
