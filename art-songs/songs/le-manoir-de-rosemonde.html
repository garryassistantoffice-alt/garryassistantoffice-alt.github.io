Le manoir de Rosemonde

The Manor of Rosemonde · Henri Duparc, Mélodie
Poem: Robert de Bonnières (1850–1905)

De sa dent soudaine et vorace,
Comme un chien l'amour m'a mordu...
En suivant mon sang répandu,
Va, tu pourras suivre ma trace...
With its sudden, ravenous tooth,
love has bitten me like a dog...
By following my spilt blood,
go, you will be able to follow my track...
Prends un cheval de bonne race,
Pars, et suis mon chemin ardu,
Fondrière ou sentier perdu,
Si la course ne te harasse!
Take a horse of good breed,
set off, and follow my hard road,
quagmire or lost path,
if the ride does not wear you out!
En passant par où j'ai passé,
Tu verras que seul et blessé
J'ai parcouru ce triste monde,
Passing where I have passed,
you will see that, alone and wounded,
I have travelled through this sad world,
Et qu'ainsi je m'en fus mourir
Bien loin, bien loin, sans découvrir
Le bleu manoir de Rosemonde.
and that so I went away to die
far, far away, without finding
the blue manor of Rosemonde.
About the song. A sonnet of love as a wound: the lover, bitten 'like a dog' by love, tells whoever would follow him to track his blood across the world, to the place where he went to die without ever finding the blue manor of Rosemonde, the dream that drew him on. The text here is read from two early editions of the song (1914 and 1917), which agree; Bonnières's own printing of the poem was not reached. 'Fondrière': a boggy hollow, a quagmire.
