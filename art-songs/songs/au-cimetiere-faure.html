Au cimetière

At the Cemetery · Gabriel Fauré, Op. 51, No. 2 (1888)
Poem: Jean Richepin (1849–1926), from La mer

Heureux qui meurt ici
Ainsi
Que les oiseaux des champs!
Son corps, près des amis,
Est mis
Dans l'herbe et dans les chants.
Happy he who dies here,
just
like the birds of the fields!
His body, near his friends,
is laid
in the grass and among the songs.
Il dort d'un bon sommeil
Vermeil
Sous le ciel radieux.
Tous ceux qu'il a connus,
Venus,
Lui font de longs adieux.
He sleeps a good sleep,
rosy,
under the radiant sky.
All those he knew,
having come,
bid him long farewells.
À sa croix les parents
Pleurants
Restent agenouillés;
Et ses os, sous les fleurs,
De pleurs
Sont doucement mouillés.
At his cross his kinsfolk,
weeping,
remain kneeling;
and his bones, under the flowers,
with tears
are gently moistened.
Chacun, sur le bois noir,
Peut voir
S'il était jeune ou non,
Et peut, avec de vrais
Regrets,
L'appeler par son nom.
Each one, on the black wood,
can see
whether he was young or not,
and can, with true
regret,
call him by his name.
Combien plus malchanceux
Sont ceux
Qui meurent à la mé,
Et sous le flot profond
S'en vont
Loin du pays aimé!
How much more unlucky
are those
who die at sea,
and beneath the deep waves
go away
far from the beloved land!
Ah! pauvres! qui pour seuls
Linceuls
Ont les goémons verts,
Où l'on roule inconnu,
Tout nu,
Et les yeux grands ouverts!
Ah, poor souls, who for their only
shrouds
have the green seaweed,
where one rolls, unknown,
stark naked,
and with eyes wide open!
Heureux qui meurt ici
Ainsi
Que les oiseaux des champs!
Son corps, près des amis,
Est mis
Dans l'herbe et dans les chants.
Happy he who dies here,
just
like the birds of the fields!
His body, near his friends,
is laid
in the grass and among the songs.
About the song. From Richepin's La mer (1886), in the section 'Les gas' (the lads: Breton sailors). A sailor reflects that a man buried at home is lucky beside those drowned at sea. 'La mé' is the sailors' pronunciation of 'la mer'; 'goémons' is the Breton word for seaweed (Fauré's score: 'goëmons'). The poem itself returns to its first stanza at the end; Fauré also repeats the second.
