Villanelle

Villanelle · Hector Berlioz, Les nuits d'été, Op. 7, No. 1 (1840–41)
Poem: Théophile Gautier (1811–1872)

Quand viendra la saison nouvelle,
Quand auront disparu les froids,
Tous les deux nous irons, ma belle,
Pour cueillir le muguet aux bois;
Sous nos pieds égrenant les perles
Que l'on voit au matin trembler,
Nous irons écouter les merles
Siffler.
When the new season comes,
when the cold has gone,
we two shall go, my lovely one,
to gather lily of the valley in the woods;
scattering under our feet the pearls
that one sees trembling in the morning,
we shall go and hear the blackbirds
whistle.
Le printemps est venu, ma belle,
C'est le mois des amants béni,
Et l'oiseau, satinant son aile,
Dit des vers au rebord du nid.
Oh! viens donc sur ce banc de mousse
Pour parler de nos beaux amours,
Et dis-moi de ta voix si douce:
Toujours!
Spring has come, my lovely one;
it is the month blessed by lovers,
and the bird, smoothing its wing to satin,
speaks verses on the edge of the nest.
Oh, come then to this bank of moss
to talk of our beautiful love,
and tell me in your voice so sweet:
'Forever!'
Loin, bien loin, égarant nos courses,
Faisons fuir le lapin caché,
Et le daim au miroir des sources
Admirant son grand bois penché;
Puis, chez nous, tout heureux, tout aises,
En paniers enlaçant nos doigts,
Revenons rapportant des fraises
Des bois.
Far, far away, letting our wanderings stray,
let us startle the hidden rabbit
and the buck in the mirror of the springs
admiring his great bent antlers;
then home, all happy, all at ease,
our fingers interlaced like a basket,
let us come back bringing strawberries
from the woods.
About the song. The first song of Les nuits d'été: a spring outing planned by two lovers, from the woods in the morning to the walk home at evening. Gautier called the poem 'Villanelle rhythmique'. His text reads 'au bois' (Berlioz: 'aux bois'), 'Dit des vers', 'sur le banc', 'tout joyeux', 'En panier'; one old Berlioz print (Sibley copy) has 'Faisant fuir' where the poem and OpenScore have 'Faisons fuir'. 'Bois' means both 'woods' and a stag's antlers, and 'fraises des bois' are wild strawberries.
