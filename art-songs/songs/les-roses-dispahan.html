Les roses d'Ispahan

The Roses of Isfahan · Gabriel Fauré, Op. 39 No. 4
Poem: Charles-Marie Leconte de Lisle (1818–1894), from Poèmes tragiques

Les roses d'Ispahan dans leur gaine de mousse,
Les jasmins de Mossoul, les fleurs de l'oranger
Ont un parfum moins frais, ont une odeur moins douce,
Ô blanche Leïlah! que ton souffle léger.
The roses of Isfahan in their sheath of moss,
the jasmines of Mosul, the orange blossoms
have a scent less fresh, a fragrance less sweet,
O fair Leïlah, than your light breath.
Ta lèvre est de corail et ton rire léger
Sonne mieux que l'eau vive et d'une voix plus douce,
Mieux que le vent joyeux qui berce l'oranger,
Mieux que l'oiseau qui chante au bord d'un nid de mousse.
Your lip is coral, and your light laughter
rings better than running water, and with a sweeter voice,
better than the joyful wind that rocks the orange tree,
better than the bird that sings at the edge of a mossy nest.
Ô Leïlah! depuis que de leur vol léger
Tous les baisers ont fui de ta lèvre si douce,
Il n'est plus de parfum dans le pâle oranger,
Ni de céleste arôme aux roses dans leur mousse.
O Leïlah, since in their light flight
all the kisses have fled from your lips so sweet,
there is no more scent in the pale orange tree,
nor heavenly fragrance in the roses in their moss.
Oh! que ton jeune amour, ce papillon léger,
Revienne vers mon cœur d'une aile prompte et douce,
Et qu'il parfume encor la fleur de l'oranger,
Les roses d'Ispahan dans leur gaine de mousse!
Oh, may your young love, that light butterfly,
come back to my heart on swift and gentle wing,
and perfume once more the orange blossom,
the roses of Isfahan in their sheath of moss!
About the song. An oriental love poem in which every scent and sound is weaker than Leïlah's breath and laughter, until her kisses fly away. Fauré sets four of the poet's six stanzas (he leaves out the third and fifth). Fauré's text differs from the poem in two places, where both the OpenScore transcription and the Ditson edition (1904) agree: 'd'un nid' (a nest) for the poet's 'du nid' (the nest), and in the last stanza 'la fleur de l'oranger' (the orange blossom) for 'les fleurs'. In the third stanza OpenScore reads 'parfums' (plural) while the Ditson score has the poet's 'parfum'; since the two scores we could read disagree, we print the poem's reading, 'parfum'.
