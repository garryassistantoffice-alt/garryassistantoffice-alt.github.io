In der Ferne

Far Away · Franz Schubert, Schwanengesang, D. 957, No. 6 (1828)
Poem: Ludwig Rellstab (1799–1860)

Wehe dem Fliehenden,
Welt hinaus ziehenden! –
Fremde durchmessenden,
Heimat vergessenden,
Mutterhaus hassenden,
Freunde verlassenden
Folget kein Segen, ach!
Auf ihren Wegen nach!
Woe to the one who flees,
who goes out into the world,
who crosses foreign lands,
who forgets his homeland,
who hates his mother's house,
who abandons his friends:
no blessing, alas, follows such men
on their way!
Herze, das sehnende,
Auge, das tränende,
Sehnsucht, nie endende,
Heimwärts sich wendende!
Busen, der wallende,
Klage, verhallende,
Abendstern, blinkender,
Hoffnungslos sinkender!
The heart, yearning,
the eye, weeping,
longing, never ending,
turning towards home!
The breast, heaving,
lament, dying away,
evening star, twinkling,
sinking without hope!
Lüfte, ihr säuselnden,
Wellen, sanft kräuselnden,
Sonnenstrahl, eilender,
Nirgend verweilender:
Die mir mit Schmerze, ach!
Dies treue Herze brach –
Grüßt von dem Fliehenden,
Welt hinaus ziehenden!
You whispering breezes,
you gently rippling waves,
you hurrying sunbeam,
lingering nowhere:
to her who, with pain, alas,
broke this faithful heart,
bring greetings from the one who flees,
who goes out into the world!
About the song. A curse and a lament in short, falling phrases: no blessing follows those who leave home, mother and friends for the wide world; the exile can only ask the breezes, waves and sunbeams to carry his greeting to the woman who broke his heart. Almost every line is a participle used as a noun or adjective ('Fliehenden', the one fleeing; 'Welt hinaus ziehenden', going out into the world). Schubert repeats the whole last stanza; we print it once.
