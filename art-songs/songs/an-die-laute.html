An die Laute

To the Lute · Franz Schubert, D. 905, Op. 81, No. 2 (1827)
Poem: Friedrich Rochlitz (1769–1842)

Leiser, leiser, kleine Laute,
flüstre, was ich dir vertraute,
dort zu jenem Fenster hin!
Wie die Wellen sanfter Lüfte
Mondenglanz und Blumendüfte,
send' es der Gebieterin!
Softer, softer, little lute,
whisper what I confided to you
over there, to that window!
As waves of gentle breezes carry
moonlight and the scent of flowers,
send it to my lady!
Neidisch sind des Nachbars Söhne,
und im Fenster jener Schöne
flimmert noch ein einsam Licht.
Drum noch leiser, kleine Laute:
dich vernehme die Vertraute,
Nachbarn aber, Nachbarn nicht!
The neighbour's sons are envious,
and in the window of that beauty
a lonely light still flickers.
So softer still, little lute:
let the one I trust hear you,
but the neighbours, the neighbours not!
About the song. A night serenade: the lover tells his lute to play softly so that only his beloved, and not the envious neighbours, will hear. Rochlitz's earlier version (1805) ended 'Nachbar nur und Mutter nicht' (only not the neighbour and her mother); Schubert sets the later text printed in 1822. 'Gebieterin' is literally 'the woman who commands', the lady the lover serves.
