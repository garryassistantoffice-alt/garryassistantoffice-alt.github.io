Litanei auf das Fest Allerseelen

Litany for the Feast of All Souls · Franz Schubert, D. 343 (published after Schubert's death)
Poem: Johann Georg Jacobi (1740–1814)

Ruh'n in Frieden alle Seelen,
Die vollbracht ein banges Quälen,
Die vollendet süßen Traum,
Lebenssatt, geboren kaum,
Aus der Welt hinüberschieden:
Alle Seelen ruh'n in Frieden!
May all souls rest in peace,
those who have come through anxious torment,
those who have finished a sweet dream,
weary of life, or barely born,
who have passed over out of the world:
may all souls rest in peace!
Liebevoller Mädchen Seelen,
Deren Tränen nicht zu zählen,
Die ein falscher Freund verließ,
Und die blinde Welt verstieß:
Alle, die von hinnen schieden,
Alle Seelen ruh'n in Frieden!
The souls of loving girls
whose tears cannot be counted,
whom a false lover abandoned
and the blind world cast out:
all who have departed from here,
may all souls rest in peace!
Und die nie der Sonne lachten,
Unterm Mond auf Dornen wachten,
Gott im reinen Himmelslicht
Einst zu sehn von Angesicht:
Alle, die von hinnen schieden,
Alle Seelen ruh'n in Frieden!
And those who never smiled in the sun,
who lay awake on thorns beneath the moon,
to see God in the pure light of heaven
one day face to face:
all who have departed from here,
may all souls rest in peace!
About the song. Jacobi's poem has nine stanzas, the last repeating the first; the song is sung with three of them (the poem's 1st, 3rd and 6th), the selection of the first edition, published after Schubert's death (whether Schubert or his publisher Diabelli chose them is not known). Jacobi added a note to the poem: on this feast Catholics visit the graves of their families, set lights on them and pray for the dead. 'Freund' here is the girl's lover, not just a friend.
