Die Wetterfahne

The Weathervane · Franz Schubert, Winterreise, D. 911, No. 2 (1827)
Poem: Wilhelm Müller (1794–1827)

Der Wind spielt mit der Wetterfahne
Auf meines schönen Liebchens Haus.
Da dacht' ich schon in meinem Wahne,
Sie pfiff' den armen Flüchtling aus.
The wind is playing with the weathervane
on my pretty sweetheart's house.
In my delusion I thought even then
that it was jeering at the poor fugitive.
Er hätt' es eher bemerken sollen,
Des Hauses aufgestecktes Schild,
So hätt' er nimmer suchen wollen
Im Haus ein treues Frauenbild.
He should have noticed it sooner,
the sign set up on the house;
then he would never have thought of looking
for a faithful woman in that house.
Der Wind spielt drinnen mit den Herzen
Wie auf dem Dach, nur nicht so laut.
Was fragen sie nach meinen Schmerzen?
Ihr Kind ist eine reiche Braut.
Inside, the wind plays with the hearts
as it does on the roof, only not so loudly.
What do they care about my pain?
Their child is a rich bride.
About the song. The traveller looks back at his sweetheart's house and sees the weathervane on its roof turning in the wind: a sign, he now thinks, that the people inside are as changeable as the vane, and that their daughter has been married off for money. 'Sie pfiff'' is subjunctive ('as if it were whistling'), and to 'whistle someone out' (auspfeifen) is to jeer at him. Müller's poem has 'eh'r' for 'eher'.
