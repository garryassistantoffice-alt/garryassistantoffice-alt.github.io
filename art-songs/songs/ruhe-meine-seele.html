Ruhe, meine Seele!

Rest, My Soul! · Richard Strauss, Op. 27, No. 1 (1894)
Poem: Karl Henckell (1864–1929)

Nicht ein Lüftchen regt sich leise,
sanft entschlummert ruht der Hain;
durch der Blätter dunkle Hülle
stiehlt sich lichter Sonnenschein.
Not a breath of wind stirs, however softly,
the grove rests, gently fallen asleep;
through the dark veil of the leaves
bright sunshine steals its way.
Ruhe, ruhe, meine Seele,
deine Stürme gingen wild,
hast getobt und hast gezittert,
wie die Brandung, wenn sie schwillt!
Rest, rest, my soul,
your storms ran wild,
you have raged and you have trembled
like the surf when it swells!
Diese Zeiten sind gewaltig,
bringen Herz und Hirn in Not –
ruhe, ruhe, meine Seele,
und vergiss, was dich bedroht!
These times are overwhelming,
they bring heart and mind into distress;
rest, rest, my soul,
and forget what threatens you!
About the song. The first of the four songs Strauss published as Op. 27 in 1894; over hushed, wandering chords a still summer grove becomes an image of the calm the troubled soul needs. 'Brandung' is the surf breaking on a shore; 'gewaltig' means immense, overwhelming; 'Hirn' is literally the brain. Strauss sings 'und vergiss' twice at the end; we print it once.
