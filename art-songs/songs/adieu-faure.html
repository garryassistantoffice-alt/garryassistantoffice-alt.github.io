Adieu

Farewell · Gabriel Fauré, Poème d'un jour, Op. 21, No. 3
Poem: Charles Grandmougin (1850–1930)

Comme tout meurt vite, la rose
Déclose,
Et les frais manteaux diaprés
Des prés;
Les longs soupirs, les bien-aimées,
Fumées!
How quickly everything dies: the rose
in bloom,
and the fresh, many-coloured mantles
of the meadows;
long sighs, beloved women:
smoke!
On voit dans ce monde léger
Changer
Plus vite que les flots des grèves
Nos rêves,
Plus vite que le givre en fleurs
Nos cœurs!
In this fickle world we see
change,
faster than the waves on the shore,
our dreams,
faster than frost in flower,
our hearts!
À vous l'on se croyait fidèle,
Cruelle,
Mais hélas! les plus longs amours
Sont courts!
Et je dis en quittant vos charmes,
Sans larmes,
Presqu'au moment de mon aveu,
Adieu!
One thought oneself faithful to you,
cruel one,
but alas, the longest loves
are short!
And I say, as I leave your charms,
without tears,
almost at the moment I declared my love,
farewell!
About the song. The last song of 'Poème d'un jour': everything fades, roses, meadows, sighs and hearts, and the lover takes his leave without tears. Each long line is answered by a short rhyming echo of two or three syllables. 'Déclose': opened, in full bloom; 'diaprés': many-coloured, dappled; 'aveu': declaration of love.
