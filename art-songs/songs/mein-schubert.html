Mein!

Mine! · Franz Schubert, Die schöne Müllerin, D. 795, No. 11 (1823)
Poem: Wilhelm Müller (1794–1827)

Bächlein, laß dein Rauschen sein!
Räder, stellt eu'r Brausen ein!
All' ihr muntern Waldvögelein,
Groß und klein,
Endet eure Melodein!
Durch den Hain
Aus und ein
Schalle heut' ein Reim allein:
Die geliebte Müllerin ist mein!
Mein!
Little brook, stop your rushing!
Wheels, cease your roaring!
All you merry little woodland birds,
great and small,
end your melodies!
Through the grove,
in and out,
let one rhyme alone ring out today:
the beloved miller's daughter is mine!
Mine!
Frühling, sind das alle deine Blümelein?
Sonne, hast du keinen hellern Schein?
Ach, so muß ich ganz allein
Mit dem seligen Worte mein
Unverstanden in der weiten Schöpfung sein!
Spring, are these all the flowers you have?
Sun, have you no brighter light?
Ah, then I must be all alone
with that blissful word, mine,
not understood in the whole wide creation!
About the song. He believes he has won her and wants the brook, the mill-wheels and the birds to fall silent so that a single rhyme can ring out: she is mine. In the second part he finds the whole of spring too small for his joy. Müller prints 'mein' spaced out for emphasis; 'mit dem seligen Worte mein' means 'with that blissful word, mine'. Schubert repeats the first section at the end.
