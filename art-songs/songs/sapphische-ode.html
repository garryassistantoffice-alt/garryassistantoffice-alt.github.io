Sapphische Ode

Sapphic Ode · Johannes Brahms, Op. 94, No. 4 (1884)
Poem: Hans Schmidt (1854–1923)

Rosen brach ich nachts mir am dunklen Hage;
Süßer hauchten Duft sie, als je am Tage;
Doch verstreuten reich die bewegten Äste
Tau, der mich näßte.
I picked roses at night by the dark hedge;
they breathed a sweeter scent than ever by day;
but the stirring branches scattered
a wealth of dew that wet me.
Auch der Küsse Duft mich wie nie berückte,
Die ich nachts vom Strauch deiner Lippen pflückte:
Doch auch dir, bewegt im Gemüt gleich jenen,
Tauten die Tränen.
The scent of the kisses, too, beguiled me as never before,
the kisses I picked at night from the bush of your lips:
but from you as well, stirred in your heart like the branches,
tears fell like dew.
About the song. Two matching images: roses picked at night smell sweeter than by day but shower the poet with dew; kisses taken at night are sweeter too, but bring the beloved's tears. The title refers to the classical stanza form named after Sappho, which Schmidt imitates in rhyme. 'Hag' is a hedge, and 'berückte' means beguiled, enchanted.
