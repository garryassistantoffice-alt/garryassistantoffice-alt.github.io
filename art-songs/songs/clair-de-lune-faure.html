Clair de lune

Moonlight · Gabriel Fauré, Op. 46 No. 2
Poem: Paul Verlaine (1844–1896), from Fêtes galantes

Votre âme est un paysage choisi
Que vont charmant masques et bergamasques,
Jouant du luth et dansant, et quasi
Tristes sous leurs déguisements fantasques.
Your soul is a chosen landscape
which masquers and bergamask dancers go about charming,
playing the lute and dancing, and almost
sad beneath their fanciful disguises.
Tout en chantant sur le mode mineur
L'amour vainqueur et la vie opportune,
Ils n'ont pas l'air de croire à leur bonheur,
Et leur chanson se mêle au clair de lune,
While they sing in the minor key
of conquering love and a life of good fortune,
they do not seem to believe in their happiness,
and their song mingles with the moonlight,
Au calme clair de lune triste et beau,
Qui fait rêver les oiseaux dans les arbres,
Et sangloter d'extase les jets d'eau,
Les grands jets d'eau sveltes parmi les marbres.
with the calm moonlight, sad and beautiful,
that sets the birds dreaming in the trees
and makes the fountains sob with ecstasy,
the tall slender fountains among the marble statues.
About the song. The opening poem of Verlaine's Fêtes galantes, a world of masked revellers in a moonlit park. Fauré subtitled the song 'Menuet': the piano plays a minuet over which the voice seems to drift. 'Bergamasques' are dancers of the bergamasca (from Bergamo); the score reads 'Que vont charmant' (go about charming; the 1900 Verlaine edition prints 'charmants', which would make the masquers 'charming'), and 'la vie opportune' is literally 'the opportune life', probably 'a life of good fortune'.
