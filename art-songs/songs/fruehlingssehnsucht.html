Frühlingssehnsucht

Spring Longing · Franz Schubert, Schwanengesang, D. 957, No. 3 (1828)
Poem: Ludwig Rellstab (1799–1860)

Säuselnde Lüfte
Wehend so mild,
Blumiger Düfte
Atmend erfüllt!
Wie haucht ihr mich wonnig begrüßend an!
Wie habt ihr dem pochenden Herzen getan?
Es möchte euch folgen auf luftiger Bahn!
Wohin?
Whispering breezes,
blowing so gently,
filled with the breath
of flowery scents!
How blissfully you breathe your greeting on me!
What have you done to my pounding heart?
It would like to follow you on your airy path!
Where to?
Bächlein, so munter
Rauschend zumal,
Wollen hinunter
Silbern ins Tal.
Die schwebende Welle, dort eilt sie dahin!
Tief spiegeln sich Fluren und Himmel darin.
Was ziehst du mich, sehnend verlangender Sinn,
Hinab?
Little brooks, so merry,
all rushing together,
want to go down,
silver, into the valley.
The hovering wave, there it hurries away!
Fields and sky are mirrored deep within it.
Why do you draw me, yearning, longing mind,
downward?
Grüßender Sonne
Spielendes Gold,
Hoffende Wonne
Bringest du hold!
Wie labt mich dein selig begrüßendes Bild!
Es lächelt am tiefblauen Himmel so mild
Und hat mir das Auge mit Tränen gefüllt!
Warum?
Playing gold
of the greeting sun,
you graciously bring
hopeful bliss!
How your blissful, greeting image refreshes me!
It smiles so gently in the deep blue sky
and has filled my eyes with tears!
Why?
Grünend umkränzet
Wälder und Höh'!
Schimmernd erglänzet
Blütenschnee!
So dränget sich alles zum bräutlichen Licht;
Es schwellen die Keime, die Knospe bricht;
Sie haben gefunden, was ihnen gebricht:
Und du?
Woods and hills
are wreathed in green!
Snow of blossom
gleams and shimmers!
So everything presses toward the bridal light;
the seeds swell, the bud bursts;
they have found what they lacked:
and you?
Rastloses Sehnen!
Wünschendes Herz,
Immer nur Tränen,
Klage und Schmerz?
Auch ich bin mir schwellender Triebe bewußt!
Wer stillet mir endlich die drängende Lust?
Nur du befreist den Lenz in der Brust,
Nur du!
Restless longing!
Yearning heart,
always only tears,
lament and pain?
I too feel swelling desires in me!
Who will at last still my urgent longing?
Only you can set free the spring in my breast,
only you!
About the song. Breezes, brooks, sunlight and blossom all find what they long for in spring; each verse ends with a short question, and only the last answers it: only 'you' can free the spring in the singer's breast. 'Zumal' here means all together, all at once; 'gebricht' (from 'gebrechen') means is lacking. Rellstab wrote 'Wallen hinunter' (flow down); both Schubert prints have 'wollen hinunter' (want to go down). Schubert sings each closing question twice; we print it once.
