Весенние воды

Spring Waters · Sergei Rachmaninoff, Twelve Romances, Op. 14 No. 11 (1896)
Poem: Fyodor Tyutchev (1803–1873)

Ещё в полях белеет снег,
А воды уж весной шумят —
Бегут и будят сонный брег,
Бегут, и блещут, и гласят...
The snow still shows white in the fields,
but the waters already roar with spring —
they run and wake the sleeping bank,
they run and sparkle and proclaim...
Они гласят во все концы:
«Весна идёт, весна идёт!
Мы молодой весны гонцы,
Она нас выслала вперёд!»
They proclaim to every quarter:
'Spring is coming, spring is coming!
We are the messengers of young spring,
she has sent us on ahead!'
Весна идёт, весна идёт!
И тихих, тёплых майских дней
Румяный, светлый хоровод
Толпится весело за ней!
Spring is coming, spring is coming!
And the rosy, bright round dance
of quiet, warm May days
crowds merrily behind her!
About the song. Meltwater rushes through fields still white with snow, announcing that spring is on its way with a round dance of warm May days behind her. We print Tyutchev's words from his own editions (1854, 1868); no score with Rachmaninoff's Russian text could be checked, so small differences in what he set cannot be ruled out. 'Брег' is the old poetic form of 'берег' (bank, shore), and 'гласят' is an archaic word for proclaim. The 'хоровод' is a Russian round dance.
