Halt!

Stop! · Franz Schubert, Die schöne Müllerin, D. 795, No. 3 (1823)
Poem: Wilhelm Müller (1794–1827)

Eine Mühle seh' ich blinken
Aus den Erlen heraus,
Durch Rauschen und Singen
Bricht Rädergebraus.
I see a mill gleaming
out from among the alders;
through the rushing and singing
breaks the roar of wheels.
Ei willkommen, ei willkommen,
Süßer Mühlengesang!
Und das Haus, wie so traulich!
Und die Fenster, wie blank!
Oh, welcome, welcome,
sweet song of the mill!
And the house, how snug!
And the windows, how shining!
Und die Sonne, wie helle
Vom Himmel sie scheint!
Ei, Bächlein, liebes Bächlein,
War es also gemeint?
And the sun, how brightly
it shines from the sky!
Well, little brook, dear little brook,
is this what you meant?
About the song. The brook leads the young man to a mill among the alders. Müller wrote 'blicken' (peeping out); Schubert's 'blinken' (gleaming) is listed as his change in Friedlaender's notes to the Peters edition. The last line, 'War es also gemeint?' (so this is what you meant?), is taken up as the first line of the next song.
