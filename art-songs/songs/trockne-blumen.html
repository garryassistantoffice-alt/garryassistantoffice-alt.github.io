Trockne Blumen

Withered Flowers · Franz Schubert, Die schöne Müllerin, D. 795, No. 18 (1823)
Poem: Wilhelm Müller (1794–1827)

Ihr Blümlein alle,
Die sie mir gab,
Euch soll man legen
Mit mir ins Grab.
All you little flowers
that she gave me,
you shall be laid
with me in the grave.
Wie seht ihr alle
Mich an so weh,
Als ob ihr wüßtet,
Wie mir gescheh'?
Why do you all look at me
so sorrowfully,
as if you knew
what has befallen me?
Ihr Blümlein alle,
Wie welk, wie blaß?
Ihr Blümlein alle,
Wovon so naß?
All you little flowers,
why so wilted, so pale?
All you little flowers,
why so wet?
Ach, Tränen machen
Nicht maiengrün,
Machen tote Liebe
Nicht wieder blüh'n.
Ah, tears do not make things
green as May,
do not make dead love
bloom again.
Und Lenz wird kommen,
Und Winter wird geh'n,
Und Blümlein werden
Im Grase steh'n.
And spring will come,
and winter will go,
and little flowers will
stand in the grass.
Und Blümlein liegen
In meinem Grab,
Die Blümlein alle,
Die sie mir gab.
And little flowers lie
in my grave,
all the little flowers
that she gave me.
Und wenn sie wandelt
Am Hügel vorbei
Und denkt im Herzen:
Der meint' es treu!
And when she walks
past the mound
and thinks in her heart:
'He was true to me!'
Dann Blümlein alle,
Heraus, heraus!
Der Mai ist kommen,
Der Winter ist aus.
Then, all you little flowers,
come out, come out!
May has come,
winter is over.
About the song. The flowers she once gave him have dried, and he wants them buried with him; one day, when she walks past his grave and thinks that he was true, they will spring up again and it will be May. 'Der meint' es treu' means 'he meant it truly, he was sincere'; 'maiengrün' is the fresh green of May.
